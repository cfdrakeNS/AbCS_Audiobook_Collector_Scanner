## Full About page copy

### Site Title (H1)

About Aurora Accessibility

### Paragraph — opening / mission

Aurora Accessibility develops software for people who rely on screen readers, keyboard navigation, and large print. Our goal is practical tools that work on day one — without visual clutter, guesswork, or workarounds that only make sense if you can see the screen.

We are longtime accessibility practitioners, now semi-retired from full-time consulting, making established products available to a wider audience. AbCS is offered as free, source-available software. Talk3270 continues as a commercial solution for organizations that use IBM 3270-style terminal applications.

### Main Heading (H2)

What we build

### Subheading (H3)

AbCS — Audio Book Collector Scanner

### Paragraph

AbCS helps audiobook collectors organize large libraries: scan folders, import metadata, search and filter collections, and manage books with full keyboard and screen reader support. It is designed for users with low vision and for anyone who prefers not to depend on mouse-driven interfaces.

### Subheading (H3)

Talk3270

### Paragraph

Talk3270 is a set of JAWS screen reader scripts for IBM AS/400 and similar 3270/5250 terminal environments. It lets organizations customize what is spoken on terminal screens — field labels, messages, screen titles, and selected text — so employees who are blind or have low vision can work effectively in legacy business applications.

Talk3270 is sold to businesses. There is no public download; interested organizations can request a demo through our Talk3270 site.

### Main Heading (H2)

Our story

### Paragraph

Talk3270 began in the late 1990s when our lead developer created scripting for a national bank branch in Halifax, Nova Scotia. Three blind employees worked as client service representatives on standard terminal software. The bank’s head office needed a solution that could be installed at branch locations and supported with on-site training.

The developer partnered with Dominic, who had already spent many years in the accessibility field. Dominic owns Sudata Consulting, based in Toronto, and has served large employers — including banks and government agencies — since the late 1980s. His work includes workplace accommodation assessments, training, progress reporting, and documentation for employers.

Together they refined Talk3270, traveled to customer sites for installation and training, and built a product used by several major banks in Canada and the United States. That same partnership continues today.

AbCS grew from a separate personal need: managing a large audiobook collection with the same standards we apply to commercial accessibility work — keyboard access, predictable screen reader behavior, and clear structure throughout.

### Main Heading (H2)

Our approach

### List (or bullet paragraphs)

- Test with screen readers, not just automated checklists.
- Design for keyboard-first use; mouse support is optional, not required.
- Keep interfaces readable at enlarged scale and in high-contrast themes where the product supports them.
- Write help documentation that stands on its own for non-visual users. Dominic leads documentation and user-facing guides for AbCS.
- Prefer plain language over marketing language.
- We share what we learn: building AbCS showed that most Python desktop apps aren't built with screen reader users in mind, so we publish a free reference project of accessible PySide6 UI patterns for other developers (pyside6-accessible-ui-reference on GitHub: https://github.com/cfdrakeNS/pyside6-accessible-ui-reference).
